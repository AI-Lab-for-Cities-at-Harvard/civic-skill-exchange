# Browsing the catalogue: a compact view, paging, and curated collections

**Issue:** [#208](https://github.com/AI-Lab-for-Cities-at-Harvard/civic-skill-exchange/issues/208) — a compact catalogue view, with pagination
**Issue:** [#209](https://github.com/AI-Lab-for-Cities-at-Harvard/civic-skill-exchange/issues/209) — featured listings and curated collections
**Status:** ruled, 2026-10-20; see [ADR 0008](../adr/0008-the-browse-page-compact-rows-and-collections.md)
**Date:** 2026-10-20. Written against `0c015c7` (main, with #299, #306's ruling, #314 and ADR 0007 merged). The prototypes are local branches, measured and never pushed.

The owner asked for the two to be designed together, because both change what a reader sees first on the browse page. This document designs them as one page.

## What is true now

Read from `site/src/App.tsx`, `SkillCard.tsx`, `PluginCard.tsx`, `Facets.tsx`, `lib/filter.ts` and `lib/route.ts` at `0c015c7`.

- **One grid, every listing a full card.** `results.map(SkillCard)` in a `.grid` of `minmax(16.5rem, 1fr)` columns: three across at desktop, one at a phone. A card is up to five badges, the name, the namespace, the whole description, a category and scope line, and "View this skill →". Nothing is paged or truncated.
- **Plugins are a second section below the skills** (ADR 0005 decision 6, ruling on #206), with their own heading, intro, count and empty notice. Since #299, search and every facet apply to them; a plugin matches scope or localization when any one of its skills does. The facets' options and counts stay about skills.
- **The hero says "N skills · N plugins" and nothing else** (#306).
- **No browse state is in the URL.** Filters are `useState` in `App`. `parseRoute` reads a query only for `#/submit`; `#/?category=hr` falls through to a plain browse page. A filtered view cannot be linked or survive a reload, and Back leaves the page.
- **The whole results section is a live region.** `<section id="results" aria-live="polite">` wraps the count, the grid and the plugins. Every filter change asks a screen reader to announce whatever changed inside it: in principle, the whole new grid. Only the count needs announcing.
- **On a phone the facets come first, and they are long.** Below 1024 px the filters stack above the results. At 375 px, the first listing starts **3,024 px** down the page: six facets, all open, before anything to choose from.
- **Hash routing has one `#`.** An in-page anchor such as `href="#plugins-heading"` is read by the router as a route and resets the browse page. The About page and the guide use `#/about/{section}` for this reason; anything on browse that jumps within the page has to be a button.
- **The primitives of ADR 0007 are ruled but not on main.** `Stack` and `Cluster` arrive with #316, and browse is step 5 of the migration (#319). New browse UI is built on them, so the prototypes below copy the #316 foundation from `spike/310-primitives`.
- **The size gate.** The English entry chunk is 305,150 B (298.0 kB) against a recorded 304,303 B and a 3% margin: the limit is 313,432 B, so **8,282 B of headroom** today.

### From earlier analyses

- **The end-to-end spike (#286).** I-1, multi-word search finding nothing, and I-7, the Plugins section ignoring search and the counts leaving plugins out, are fixed by #299 and #306. What that spike did not look at is length: its catalogue had six skills. Neither newcomer scrolled.
- **The HBS spike (#101).** Rulings that bind here: solid status pills with black or white text; the 4px focus ring at 1px offset; the 4px radius; **disclaimers are a distinct box in italic at weight 300 (ruling 6)**, which is the `.notice` class; crimson means brand and validation, through `--c-spot`.
- **ADR 0001 decision 2** and **ADR 0002 decision 3.** A Lab listing is marked as such *wherever it appears*, and an unreviewed Lab listing shows the Community chip *with its note*. A compact row that drops either breaks a ruling, not a preference.
- **ADR 0002 decision 4.** "Curated", "official" and "first-party" were rejected as chip wording because they imply a standard that is not defined. That bears directly on what #209 may call itself.
- **ADR 0004 decision 3.** The registry does not model related skills: no pointer fields between listings. A collection is not one. It is a separate maintainer-owned file that names listings, the way `reviewed.yml` does, and no listing carries a field about it.

## #208, question 1: compact how?

### (a) A list view beside the card grid, as a toggle

A "Cards / List" radio pair above the count. List is one row per listing: name and namespace, tier, category, and the description clamped to one line (two at a phone) by CSS, so the whole text stays in the DOM for a screen reader and for find-in-page.

- **Density:** 140 px a skill at 375 px, a third of a card. At desktop it is less dense than (c), because each row carries a line of description.
- **Cost:** +2,086 B, the most of the three: two renderings ship, and the view becomes state that must be remembered (in the URL, or in `localStorage` beside the theme) or every reload resets it.
- **Accessibility:** axe clean in both themes. The radio group needs no ARIA. The one-line clamp cuts mid-word ("In plain languag…"), and a sighted reader cannot see where the description ends without opening the page.
- **Critique** (`design-critique`):
  - *What draws the eye first* is the long run of tier pills down the middle, not the names. Acceptable in a list; it is a scan by tier.
  - *Moderate:* a toggle is a decision the reader makes before they know they need it. Most readers never change a default; the ones who need density most, on a phone, are the least likely to find a small control above the count.
  - *Moderate:* two layouts double what has to stay right: each new field, badge or ruling has to be applied to both, and the tests have to cover both.
  - *What works:* the truncated description is the best scanning aid of the three options. A reader can rule a listing out from its first line without opening anything.

### (b) Denser cards by default

The same card, tighter: smaller padding and title, the description clamped to two lines, the badge notes and the "View this skill" line hidden, narrower columns. CSS only.

- **Density:** 237 px a skill at 375 px: better than today, worst of the three. At desktop it fits four across.
- **Cost:** 0 B of JS, +387 B of CSS.
- **Critique:**
  - *Critical, by ruling:* hiding `.badge__note` drops "automated checks only" from the Community chip, which ADR 0002 decision 3 requires on an unreviewed Lab listing.
  - *Moderate:* the badges become the card. With the description clamped, an unreviewed Lab skill with four chips is mostly chips, and names no longer line up across a row because badge rows wrap to different heights (the reserved `min-height` had to go to save space).
  - *Moderate:* the reader still scrolls past descriptions they have dismissed. This is the problem #208 was filed for, made smaller rather than solved.
  - *What works:* nothing new to learn, nothing to toggle, nothing to test twice.

### (c) Collapsible cards (the owner's option, 2026-10-19)

A single column of rows. Collapsed, a row is the owner's minimum on one line at desktop: name (the link to the listing's page), namespace, tier, category, and a "Details" button. Opened, the row grows in place to show the rest of today's card: the other badges, the description, scope and the call to action. Skills and plugins both.

- **Density:** 132 px a skill at 375 px, 79 at desktop. The densest option, by a little over (a).
- **Cost:** +606 B, including the first use of the primitives.
- **Accessibility:** axe clean in both themes, the jsdom a11y and keyboard suites pass. The toggle is a `<button aria-expanded aria-controls>` named "Details" plus the listing's name in visually hidden text, so the reader hears "Details, council-compare, collapsed, button" and a list of twelve buttons is not twelve identical "Details". The opened content follows the button in DOM order, so Tab moves into it.
- **Two defects the prototype found:**
  - `hidden` did nothing at first. A primitive sets `display: flex` in the `layout` layer, which beats the browser's own `[hidden] { display: none }`. The fix is one rule in `layout.css`, `.l-stack[hidden], .l-cluster[hidden] { display: none; }`; #316 should carry it, or every disclosure built on a primitive fails the same way.
  - The heading inside a row has to take its level as a prop. The prototype used `h3`; on a collection's page, under its `h1`, axe reported `heading-order`.
- **Critique:**
  - *What draws the eye first:* the names, then the green Reviewed pills. Correct: the name is what a reader recognises, the tier is what they trust.
  - *Critical, by ruling (fixed in the recommendation, not in the prototype):* the prototype hid the Lab chip and the Community note in the collapsed row. ADR 0001 decision 2 and ADR 0002 decision 3 require both wherever a listing appears. The collapsed row must keep **the tier chip with its note, and the Lab chip when it applies**.
  - *Moderate:* the "Details" toggle is 71 × 38 px. That clears WCAG 2.2's 24 px minimum (2.5.8) but not the 44 px this site's pager and buttons reach; it should be the full row height at a phone.
  - *Moderate:* opening a card in the middle of a list moves everything below it by about 250 px. Acceptable because it is the reader's own action, and the open card's border darkens to show which one moved.
  - *Minor:* "Expand all" is the obvious next request. All open is 25,093 px at a phone, about today's page; it would be a way back to the full catalogue, not a new view.
  - *What works:* one layout, not two. The reader dismisses a listing by not opening it, which is the behaviour #208 asks for.

**Description: truncated or dropped (question 3).** In (c) it is dropped from the collapsed row and shown in full when opened. A truncated first line, as in (a), is a better scanning aid, but at a phone it doubles the row (140 vs 132 px is with it clamped to two lines in the list). Dropping it keeps the row one line at desktop; the name and category have to carry the choice, and the toggle is one press away.

## #208, question 2: paging, and the URL

Prototyped on (c), 24 skills a page.

- **Numbered pages.** A `nav` labelled "Pages", Previous, the page numbers and Next as links whose `href` is the page's own URL (`#/?page=2`), so a page can be copied or opened in a new tab; a click is handled in place. The current page is `aria-current="page"`, as the header nav does. Each link is 44 × 46 px.
- **Show more.** A button under the list appending the next 24. Nothing moves; the reader stays where they were.
- **No paging, the compact view carrying the length.** With (c), 48 skills are 4,602 px to the plugins at desktop and 9,383 at a phone. That is already less than today's 8,518 and 22,634.

| | at 48 skills | at 200 skills, extrapolated | a linkable place in the list | what the reader loses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no paging, (c) | plugins at 4,602 / 9,383 px | ≈ 16,500 / 29,500 px | no | nothing, until the list is long |
| show more | page 1 plugins at 2,834 / 6,309 | the same | no; "page 3" is not a place | Back returns to the first 24 |
| numbered | page 1 plugins at 2,834 / 6,309 | the same | yes | the plugins move to the foot of each page |

**What happens on a page change.** Focus moves to the count, which now reads "Skills 25–48 of 48" (filtered: "Skills 1–24 of the 30 that match (48 in all)"). A screen reader reads it; Tab continues into the new rows; the page scrolls to it. Back returns to the previous page: a page change pushes a history entry, a filter change replaces one, so typing in the search box does not leave forty Back steps. The prototype verified all three in the browser.

**The URL.** Filters and page move into the query of the browse route: `#/?category=hr&tier=reviewed&page=2`. `parseRoute` returns it as `{ page: "browse", query }`; a new `lib/browse.ts` turns it into filters and back, dropping any value that is not a vocabulary id, as `route.ts` already does with `SKILL_ID`. The bare `#/` is still the whole catalogue. `route.test.ts` passed unchanged. This is worth doing whichever paging is chosen: it is what makes a filtered page shareable, which is also what #209's "linkable" asks.

**Critique of paging** (`design-critique`): numbered pages are the honest option for a catalogue someone comes back to ("it was on page 2"), but at 48 skills they add a control for two pages. Show more is better on a phone and worse for return visits. Neither is needed until the compact view stops carrying the length.

## #208, question 4: facets, and the plugins section

- **A filter change resets to page 1.** The prototype does this; any other answer shows "page 3 of 1".
- **The live region moves to the count.** The prototype took `aria-live` off the section and gave each count `role="status"`. One defect remains: on a page change the focused count is also a status, so some screen readers will read it twice. The build should focus a plain heading (`tabindex="-1"`) on page change and keep `role="status"` for filter changes only.
- **Plugins do not page with skills.** They are a separate section with a separate count (ADR 0005), and there are 1 in the real catalogue and 8 in the fixture. Interleaving them into skill pages would make "page 2" mean two things. They should page on their own when they pass a page's worth, with the same control.

## #208, question 5: moving between skills and plugins

Three ways, from the owner's note of 2026-10-15:

- **A link with a count, beside the skills' count.** The prototype's "8 plugins below" sits after "Skills 1–24 of 48". Because of the hash router it is a button that moves focus to the Plugins heading, not an anchor. It costs nothing in layout, says plugins exist before the reader scrolls, and follows the filters ("1 plugin below" with Communications chosen).
- **Tabs, Skills (48) | Plugins (8).** Shows one kind at a time. It contradicts the ruling on #206 that plugins appear below the skills on the same page, and it hides plugins from a reader who never clicks the second tab, which today would be every reader.
- **A kind toggle among the facets.** Turns "skills or plugins" into a filter. Facets are about skills (ADR 0005, consequences); a kind facet would be the one facet with a different meaning.

With (c) and paging, the Plugins heading is 2,834 px down at desktop rather than 8,518, which does most of the work by itself. The link with a count does the rest.

## #209, question 1: where a feature is recorded

`registry/collections.yml`, owned by maintainers through the `/registry/` line already in `CODEOWNERS`, beside `categories.yml`. The prototype's shape:

```yaml
collections:
  - slug: generalize-and-localize      # the URL: #/collection/{slug}
    title: Moving a skill to a new place
    title_es: Llevar una habilidad a otro lugar
    note: >
      The two halves of adapting a skill written for one government: lift out
      what is local, then fill it in for yours.
    note_es: >
      …
    featured: true                     # on the browse page, or only at its own URL
    listings:
      - skill: civic-skills/generalize-skill
      - skill: civic-skills/localize-skill
```

Titles and notes in both languages sit side by side, the pattern `categories.yml` set for the vocabulary (#150). `build_index.py` publishes the file as `collections` in `index.json`, members as `{kind, id}` only, since the page already has every listing. **A member that is not in the registry fails the build**, the posture `load_attestations` takes with a malformed ledger, so a removed listing cannot leave a hole on the page.

**What the prototype found.** Five tests in `test_build_index.py` failed. They point `SKILLS_DIR` at a throwaway tree, and the prototype read `collections.yml` from the real root, where it names listings the throwaway tree does not have. It is the coupling `test_no_listing_coupling.py` exists to prevent. The build must read the file from beside whichever registry is being built, as it already does for `plugins/`, or take its path as an argument.

No backend is needed (ADR 0003): featuring is a pull request to a YAML file, reviewed like any other.

## #209, question 2: one featured list, or named collections

- **One featured list.** "Featured" with no reason given. Simple, and exactly the wording ADR 0002 decision 4 warned against: it implies a standard nobody has defined.
- **Named collections**, each with a title and a note on why its listings belong together. The note *is* the editorial claim, stated, so the reader can judge it. The `featured` flag then decides which collections the browse page shows; a collection can exist only at its own URL, for a workshop or a single department.

The second is not much more machinery than the first: a list of lists instead of a list.

## #209, question 3: does featuring say anything about quality?

**If only Reviewed listings may be featured,** a collection inherits the review's meaning without saying so, and a reader learns "in a collection" as a quality mark. It also excludes most of the catalogue: in the real registry every skill is Reviewed today, but in any registry with outside contributors most listings will not be, and a collection of tools for one task would have holes in it.

**If any listing may be,** the page must say it is not a review, and each listing keeps its own tier chip, which is already on every row. The prototype's sentence: "Chosen by the Exchange's maintainers because they work together. Being in a collection is not a review: each listing's own tier says whether it was reviewed."

`design-system` found the prototype set that sentence as a bespoke italic line; #101 ruling 6 says a disclaimer is a distinct box, `.notice`. It should be one on the collection page. On the browse page, the tiles are short enough that a one-line link to the About page's tier section may serve better than a box above every visit; that is a placement question for the build.

## #209, question 4: where it appears, and linkable

- **(A) At the top of the browse page.** Tiles above the count, on the unfiltered first page only. Once a reader has chosen a facet or typed a search, an editorial pick above the results is in the way, so the tiles go. Three tiles add 260 px at desktop and 541 at a phone.
- **(B) Its own page per collection**, `#/collection/{slug}`: title, note, the caveat, and the listings as compact rows. Linkable by construction, and the page a maintainer sends to someone.
- **Both.** The tiles are the way in; the page is the destination and the link.

**Critique** (`design-critique`, screenshots 50–52):
- *What draws the eye first* on the browse page is the collections, ahead of the catalogue. That is the point of featuring, and the reason to show them only on the unfiltered first page.
- *Critical at a phone:* the tiles sit at the top of the results, which at 375 px is below 3,024 px of facets. A phone reader sees them after scrolling past every filter. Either the facets collapse behind a "Filters" disclosure at a phone (which #208 needs anyway, below), or the tiles move above the facets on narrow screens.
- *Moderate:* the prototype's crimson top rule on each tile reads like the crimson edge of an error notice (#101 ruling 5: crimson is brand and validation). Use the neutral `--c-border` weight instead.
- *Moderate:* tiles of different note lengths did not line up. `.l-cluster` sets `align-items: center` in the `layout` layer, and the tile's own `align-items: stretch` in `legacy` cannot override it. That is ADR 0007 working as designed: alignment has to become a prop of the primitive (`align="stretch"`) when a page needs it, not a CSS override.
- *Moderate:* a collection page mixing skills and plugins showed nothing that marked the plugin as a plugin. A compact plugin row needs a "Plugin · 3 skills" marker.
- *What works:* the note under each title makes the grouping's reason visible before the click; the count ("6 listings") says how big the commitment is.

## #209, question 5: skills and plugins together?

Yes. A collection is for a task, and the task is the same whether a step is done by a single skill or by a plugin; "performance management" in the fixture has five skills and a plugin. ADR 0005 keeps the two apart in the catalogue because the facets mean different things for each, and a collection has no facets. The price is the kind marker above.

## How #208 and #209 fit on one browse page

From the top, at desktop, with nothing filtered:

```
 ┌ hero: N skills · N plugins ─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 ├ tier band ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ FILTERS        │ Collections                                            │
 │  search        │ ┌ Performance mgmt ┐ ┌ Writing for res. ┐ ┌ A permit… ┐│
 │  tier          │ │ why · 6 listings │ │ why · 4 listings │ │ why · 5   ││
 │  category      │ └──────────────────┘ └──────────────────┘ └───────────┘│
 │  …             │ Skills 1–24 of 48 · 8 plugins below                    │
 │                │ ┌ council-compare  civic-skills  Community (checks only)│
 │                │ │                  Written by the AI Lab  AI Tools  Details ▾
 │                │ ├ kpi-router       sample-county  Reviewed   Operations   Details ▾
 │                │ │ …                                                     │
 │                │ ‹ Previous  1  2  Next ›                               │
 │                │ ── Plugins ───────────────────────────────────────     │
 │                │ 8 plugins, compact rows, paged on their own past 24    │
 └────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- Filtering removes the collections, resets to page 1, rewrites the URL and announces the count.
- `#/collection/{slug}` shows one collection with the same compact rows, so a listing looks the same wherever it appears.
- At a phone, the facets need to collapse behind a "Filters" button showing the number chosen; otherwise every option here starts 3,024 px down. This is not one of #208's questions as filed, but it is the largest single measurement in this spike.

**Order of work.** #319 migrates browse to the primitives (ADR 0007 step 5). Building #208 inside it, rather than after it, means the browse page's rules are rewritten once. #209's index change can land first and independently, since the site ignores a key it does not read.

## Decision questions

**1. Compact how (#208 q1).** Recommend **(c), collapsible rows, as the only browse layout**, built inside #319 on `Stack` and `Cluster`. The collapsed row keeps the name, namespace, the tier chip *with its note*, the Lab chip when it applies, and the category. No toggle, and no second layout. Not (a), because two layouts cost three times the bytes and double the tests for density a default can give; not (b), because it shortens the problem rather than solving it.

**2. Paging (#208 q2).** Recommend **no paging yet**: the compact rows carry 48 listings in less height than today's 6. Build the URL state now; add **numbered pages of 24** when the catalogue passes about 100 skills, behind the same URL (`page=`), focusing a heading on change. Not show-more, because a reader cannot link to or return to "the third batch".

**3. The URL (#208 q2).** Recommend **filters in the browse route's query now**, `#/?category=hr&tier=reviewed`, with filters replacing the history entry and pages, when they come, pushing one. Values that are not vocabulary ids are dropped.

**4. The description in a compact row (#208 q3).** Recommend **dropped from the collapsed row, in full when opened.** Revisit a one-line truncation only if readers open rows mainly to read the first sentence.

**5. Facets and plugins (#208 q4).** Recommend: **a filter change resets to page 1; plugins page on their own**, with the same control, only when they pass a page; **the live region moves from the whole section to the counts.**

**6. Moving between skills and plugins (#208 q5).** Recommend **a link-styled button with a count beside the skills' count, "8 plugins below"**, following the filters and moving focus to the Plugins heading. Not tabs, which would hide plugins from anyone who does not click and reverse the #206 ruling; not a kind facet.

**7. Where a collection is recorded (#209 q1).** Recommend **`registry/collections.yml`**, maintainer-owned through the existing `/registry/` CODEOWNERS line, bilingual like `categories.yml`, published by `build_index.py` as `collections` with members as `{kind, id}`, failing the build on an unknown member, and **read from beside the registry being built** so the fixture-tree tests keep their isolation.

**8. One list or named collections (#209 q2).** Recommend **named collections, each with a title and a note on why its listings belong together, and a `featured` flag** for the ones the browse page shows. Call them "Collections"; never "Featured", "Curated" or "Recommended" (ADR 0002 decision 4).

**9. Quality (#209 q3).** Recommend **any listing may be in a collection, and the collection says it is not a review**, in a `.notice` on the collection page (#101 ruling 6). Every row keeps its own tier chip. Not Reviewed-only, which would make membership a second quality signal the registry never defined.

**10. Placement and links (#209 q4).** Recommend **both: tiles at the top of the unfiltered first page, and a page per collection at `#/collection/{slug}`**, the page as its own lazy chunk. The tiles hide once a filter is chosen. At a phone the tiles must not sit below the facets (question 12).

**11. Kinds together (#209 q5).** Recommend **skills and plugins in one collection**, with a "Plugin · N skills" marker on a plugin's compact row wherever rows appear.

**12. The facets at a phone (raised by the measurements, not filed).** Recommend a **"Filters" disclosure below 1024 px**, showing the number of filters chosen, so the first listing (or the collections) is in the first screen instead of 3,024 px down. File it as its own issue under #319 if accepted.

**13. Two fixes for #316, found here.** Recommend #316 carry **`.l-stack[hidden], .l-cluster[hidden] { display: none; }`** and an **`align` prop on `Cluster`**. Without the first, every disclosure built on a primitive ignores `hidden`; without the second, a page cannot stretch a row of tiles without fighting the layer order ADR 0007 set up.

## What this could not verify

- **A real screen reader.** Announcements are inferred from the DOM, ARIA and axe. Nobody listened to the page in VoiceOver or NVDA, so the double announcement in finding 2 is likely, not observed.
- **Real catalogue sizes.** The fixture is synthetic, 48 skills and 8 plugins with stock descriptions. Real descriptions vary more in length, which matters most for (b).
- **The 1280 px desktop screenshots.** The browser window gave a viewport of about 1,244 px, so the desktop screenshots are at that width. The measurements were made in iframes of exactly 1280 and 375 px.
- **The 200-skill figures.** Extrapolated from the per-row heights, not measured.
- **The issues themselves.** #208 and #209 were read from the brief, not from GitHub: there was no `gh` in this session.
- **The whole vitest suite on each branch.** It was run on the collapsible branch only: 1,541 pass, and two fail because the browse page's text changed (`rendered-text.test.tsx`'s snapshot, and the Plugins test of the "no plugins match" notice, not investigated). The others ran the a11y, keyboard and route suites.
