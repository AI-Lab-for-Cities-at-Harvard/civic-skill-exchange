# Domain pack — State labor and workforce

Tier: state. For a city or county workforce board, use this pack with the cross-tier notes at the end.

Every standard and federal source named here is background knowledge until retrieved in the run. Quote a standard only after confirming its current version.

## Typical remit (List A territory)

Where the context does not define the labor agency's remit, assume: unemployment insurance, workforce development and training (WIOA programs), labor standards and wage enforcement, employment services and career centers, registered apprenticeship, worker protection and safety for public employees, and labor market information.

States organize this differently. Workforce development sometimes sits in a commerce or economic development agency; workers' compensation is usually a separate board; some states split UI from everything else. Follow the context.

## Federal comparators

Useful when the portal lacks a series and as a cross-check when it has one. All reachable by web search.

- **US DOL Employment and Training Administration** — weekly initial and continued claims by state; the quarterly UI Data Summary; state performance on core UI measures including first payment timeliness, lower-authority appeals timeliness, and payment accuracy.
- **BLS** — LAUS, QCEW, state CES, state JOLTS estimates, and OEWS state occupational employment and wages.
- **WIOA performance** — state annual performance reports on employment, earnings and credential outcomes.
- **Registered apprenticeship data** published by US DOL.

Federal UI performance standards worth checking the current version of: first payment timeliness (the long-standing benchmark has been 87% of first payments within 14 days, or 21 in waiting-week states) and lower-authority appeals timeliness (60% within 30 days, 80% within 45). Cite them only after retrieving the current standard and the state's current figure.

## Timeliness metric

UI duration, first payment timeliness, appeals decision time, and wage claim resolution time. Duration is the most often available on state portals; the federal core measures are the most comparable across states.

## Domain traps

- **Initial claims are a flow; continued claims are a stock.** Summing continued claims across weeks counts claimant-weeks.
- **Seasonally adjusted and unadjusted series sit side by side.** Month-over-month comparisons need the adjusted series; year-over-year comparisons of the same month can use either. Say which.
- **LAUS and CES are revised annually**, typically early in the year. A figure retrieved before and after benchmarking can differ; cite the retrieval date.
- **QCEW lags by roughly two quarters.** It cannot tell you about this quarter.
- **2020–2021 is not a baseline.** Pandemic-era claims and fraud-driven claims distort any comparison that includes them.
- **Region definitions differ** between the state's own labor market regions, workforce board areas, and federal metropolitan areas. Do not compare across definitions.

## Acute signals to check

WARN notices in the last 90 days (federal WARN covers large employers; many states have their own lower-threshold laws); a week-over-week spike in initial claims; a UI system migration or modernization cutover; a large federal or state workforce reduction; a closure or strike in current reporting.

## Local media and resident signals

Local coverage tends to surface: layoffs and closures before WARN filings or claims data show them; claimants unable to reach the UI call center, stuck in identity verification, or facing overpayment demands; wage theft cases and settlements; training programs that did not place their graduates; career center closures or reduced hours. Local business press covers employer-side hiring difficulty by sector.

Resident signals, where a tool can reach them, cluster around UI claim status and call center access. They are the most direct evidence of claimant experience and the least representative.

## List B — leverage touchpoints

The labor agency touches populations and channels that other agencies struggle to reach: every UI claimant at the moment they lose income, employers at hiring and at layoff, training providers, apprenticeship sponsors, and the career center network. That makes it a distribution channel for problems owned elsewhere.

Productive territory: childcare access as a labor-force-participation constraint; transportation to job sites; benefit-cliff effects that make work irrational at the margin; health-coverage continuity at job loss; reentry employment for people leaving incarceration; credential recognition for immigrants and out-of-state licensees; housing cost near job centers; digital access for claimants filing online.

## Cross-tier notes

A city or county scan using this pack needs the state's connector for UI and LAUS by county. Where a city is coterminous with one or more counties, county series stand in for the city; say so. Where it is not, county figures are context, not city findings.
